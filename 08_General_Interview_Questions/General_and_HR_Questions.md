## A. About you

**Q1. Tell me about yourself.** → `01_EASY` E1 (90 s, Present → Past → Future).

**Q2. What are your strengths?** Pick 2, each with proof.
> "**I go deep on problems** — in LocalArena I didn't stop at 'it works', I load-tested it, broke it on purpose and wrote postmortems. And **I learn fast** — I picked up Go, Kafka Streams and Kubernetes for that project in 8 weeks."

**Q3. What is your weakness?** Real, not fatal, with improvement.
> "I sometimes over-build — LocalArena had more components than it needed. Now I start with the simplest version, time-box it, and add complexity only when a measurement says I need it."
*(Avoid: "I'm a perfectionist" / "I work too hard".)*

**Q4. How would your friends/teammates describe you?** "Reliable and curious — I'm the one who asks 'what happens if this fails?'"

**Q5. What do you do outside coding?** Mentoring at Codebase Club, building side projects, reading engineering blogs (name one from `09_Tech_Blogs`). Add one genuine non-tech hobby.

**Q6. What motivates you?** "Building things people use, and seeing a number improve because of something I changed."

## B. About the job / company

**Q7. Why do you want to work here?** → `01_EASY` E3 template + one fact from their engineering blog.

**Q8. What do you know about our company?** Product, scale, one recent tech blog post, one recent product launch. Never go in blank.

**Q9. Why backend?** → `01_EASY` E4.

**Q10. Why should we hire you?** → `04_BEHAVIORAL_STAR` closing section.

**Q11. Where do you see yourself in 3–5 years?**
> "Owning a backend service end to end — design, scaling, on-call — and mentoring newer engineers. I'd like to go deep in distributed systems and LLM serving infrastructure."

**Q12. What kind of team/work environment do you prefer?** "Teams with strong code review and ownership, where I can ask questions and also take responsibility for something real."

## C. Logistics (be clear and honest)

**Q13. Are you willing to relocate?** Answer yes/no honestly; if yes: "Yes, I'm comfortable relocating."

**Q14. Can you work from office / hybrid?** Know your answer before the call.

**Q15. When can you join? How long can you intern?** Know your exact dates, university rules (NOC), and exams. Don't promise dates you can't meet.

**Q16. Do you have other offers?** Honest and neutral: "I'm in process with a few companies, but this role is a strong fit for me because …" Don't bluff offers.

**Q17. Expected stipend/salary?**
> "I'm flexible and more focused on the role and learning. I understand the typical range for this role is around [research it on Glassdoor / Levels.fyi / peers]; I'm happy with a fair offer within that." 
Let them state a number first if possible.

**Q18. Why are you leaving / why another internship after Amazon?** "I've learned a lot at Amazon; I want to [work on X / different domain / full-time conversion opportunity] — and this role offers that."

**Q19. Can you explain the gap / overlapping dates?** Be factual: "Inbotiq was part-time alongside college."

## D. Work-style questions

**Q20. How do you handle tight deadlines?** Break work down, ship the core first, communicate early if something will slip.

**Q21. How do you handle disagreement with a teammate/senior?** "I share my reasoning with data, listen to theirs, and if we still disagree I commit to the team's decision and revisit with evidence later." (Amazon's *disagree and commit* — mention it.)

**Q22. How do you handle feedback?** → STAR S6 (code review story).

**Q23. Tell me about a time you failed.** → STAR S3 (silent reward failure).

**Q24. How do you stay up to date?** Engineering blogs (name 2 from `09_Tech_Blogs`), docs, building projects, LeetCode contests.

**Q25. How do you use AI tools when coding?**
> "For boilerplate, exploring unfamiliar APIs, and reviewing — but I verify everything: I read the code, run the tests, and I'm responsible for what I commit. I never paste code I can't explain."

**Q26. How do you prioritise when you have multiple tasks?** Impact × urgency; unblock others first; confirm priorities with the manager.

**Q27. Have you worked in a team? What was your role?** Amazon team (code reviews, design discussions), Inbotiq team, Codebase Club (lead).

---

## E. End of interview

**Q28. Do you have any questions for us?** Always ask 2:
1. "What would success look like for me in the first 3 months?"
2. "What's the biggest technical challenge the team is working on right now?"
3. "How does the team handle code review, testing and on-call?"
4. "What do you enjoy most about working here?"

**Q29. Anything else you'd like to add?** 20-second close:
> "Just that I'm really excited about this role — the work on [X] matches exactly what I've been building, and I'd love to contribute."

---

## Red flags to avoid
- Speaking badly about a previous company/team.
- Sharing confidential details (Amazon internals).
- "I don't know" with nothing after it → say "I don't know, but here's how I'd find out / reason about it."
- Over-long answers — stop at 2 minutes and ask "Should I go deeper?"
